# Contribution terms

Runmesh uses a simple deemed-acceptance process for community contributions.
There is no separate signature, email confirmation, CLA upload, or private
agreement record for an ordinary contribution.

When you submit a pull request, patch, issue attachment, documentation change,
test, design contribution, or other contribution to the official
`aloneio/runmesh` repository, you are treated as having accepted the following
terms for that contribution:

1. You have the right to submit the contribution and to grant the permissions
   below.
2. You keep copyright and attribution in the contribution.
3. You grant Runmesh and its maintainers a non-exclusive, worldwide,
   royalty-free, perpetual license to use, reproduce, modify, prepare
   derivatives of, distribute, sublicense, and include the contribution in the
   source-available Runmesh community edition and in future commercially
   licensed Runmesh editions.
4. Where legally applicable, you grant a corresponding patent license for
   patent claims necessarily infringed by your contribution or its combination
   with the Work, subject to the standard termination rule for an initiated
   patent-assertion proceeding.
5. You will not knowingly submit confidential, encumbered, or third-party
   material without the required permission and notices.

This contribution license applies to the submitted contribution. It does not
change the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE) for Runmesh itself
and it does not grant commercial-use rights to you, your employer, or any other
organization. Commercial production use, paid hosting, commercial SaaS, resale,
white-label distribution, and commercial integration still require separate
written authorization; see [COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md](COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md).

## Employer and entity contributions

If you contribute as an employee, contractor, or representative of an entity,
your submission confirms that you have authority to grant these rights or that
the relevant rights holder has authorized the submission. If that authority is
unclear, resolve it before submitting. Maintainers may request clarification or
decline a contribution when ownership, confidentiality, patent, or licensing
authority is uncertain.

This document is a project contribution policy, not legal advice, and does not
claim review by a lawyer. It is intended to make ordinary contribution easy
without requiring a separate agreement workflow.
